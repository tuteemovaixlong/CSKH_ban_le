## 2. Reconciled findings

### Confirmed current-source issues / prerequisites

1. **Handoff is conversation-scoped, not episode-scoped.** Staff transcript/reply/resolve operations use `conversation_id`; they are not atomically bound to one pending opaque handoff episode. Reply can write to a conversation without a pending handoff; resolve can affect multiple handoff rows and may report success without a matching pending item. Queue results can include resolved rows. Evidence: `retailops/http/routes.py` staff routes; `retailops/business/store.py` escalation/reply/resolve methods; `web/app.js` Desk calls. The reviews found no evidence of cross-tenant bypass: the demonstrated risk is wrong-object access within the tenant-bound store. Treat this as **P1 object/lifecycle authorization**, not a demonstrated P0 cross-tenant breach.

2. **Handoff acknowledgment and lifecycle are not durable end-to-end.** The customer UI can announce Human Mode/connection before persistence is acknowledged and suppress a persistence error. “Return to bot” is currently a client-side state change; polling can use an older resolved row when a later episode exists. A conversation TTL can remove its feedback through cascade, while expired conversations may be writable through selected staff/customer-message paths. Model a durable episode with its own ID, pending/resolved/customer-closed state, atomic transitions, explicit retention, and persistence-before-ack semantics.

3. **Escalation tool can fail open.** `request_human_support` can report escalation success despite a failed or absent persistence target, and the graph can suppress escalation errors. Do not claim a human has been connected or a case accepted until a durable target is confirmed.

4. **Tool capability checks are inconsistent.** Dispute may receive broader protocol schemas than the tools it handles; `/api/tools/execute` validates tool shape but does not enforce the full profile/task capability at the server executor. Other workers do have local allowlists, so this is not a claim that every worker is unrestricted. Enforce policy at the executor and fail closed with an explicit result.

5. **Audit attribution is client-influenced.** Staff reply/resolve accepts `staff_name` from the request and persists it as attribution. Bind principal identity from the authenticated session and store any display label separately. The audits did not substantiate cross-tenant event leakage; event kind/field allowlisting is still needed to keep internal material out of customer-facing APIs.

6. **Role provisioning and admin matrix need an explicit source of authority.** The reviewed `HEAD` and local `origin/main` differ: `platform_admin` is absent at `dd544800` but present at `bbfd11d0`, where its role permissions include Staff/Manager capabilities. On `bbfd11d0`, Manager also has P5-E `/api/admin/accounts*` and `/api/admin/audit-log` UI/API capabilities within its own tenant, subject to mutation limits including no mutation of `platform_admin`; `platform_admin` has cross-tenant scope under endpoint-specific rules, and not every endpoint requires an explicit client-supplied `tenant_id`. Google role resolution uses environment allowlists and `DEFAULT_ROLE`; an unsafe privileged default can grant broad access, and login-time synchronization may overwrite a stored membership role. Preserve Manager's existing human UI/API rights unless the owner approves a change; do not infer those rights for `manager_ops_assist`. Confirm the target ref and define safe provisioning/role-update rules before relying on the role matrix.

7. **Viewer permissions and customer-only bootstrap are endpoint-specific.** At both reviewed refs, Viewer can reach five session/customer-bound POST paths (`/api/conversations`, `/api/conversations/{id}/focus`, `/api/chat`, `/api/feedback`, `/api/staff/customer-message`); the confirmed scope is not arbitrary cross-customer access. If Viewer means read-only, deny writes server-side and test no state mutation; otherwise specify permitted writes. `openSession()` currently requests conversation data and may create a conversation before role routing. If Staff/Manager are denied customer conversation routes, the UI must skip those customer-only bootstrap calls while retaining the appropriate session/profile/provider/logout bootstrap. Cover all paths in the role/API matrix and test the selected policy directly.

8. **Existing UI mismatches:** Only the top-nav `#toggle-staff-desk` entry calls `openStaffDesk()` and loads the queue/starts polling. The Manager Console `#btn-manager-open-staff-desk` and SOP 6 handlers test `typeof refreshStaffQueue === 'function'`, but that identifier is a DOM button, not a function; these two entry points currently do not load the initial queue or start polling. Queue/count can include resolved entries; Manager order “View” reuses customer conversation focus and may fail for another customer's order; Human Mode attachment can be rejected after optimistic UI rendering; exchange quick actions send reply templates rather than executing an exchange transaction. These are current source/UI findings, not claims of live deployment behavior.

9. **TTL/retention lifecycle:** `new_conversation()` performs tenant-wide cleanup of expired conversations when a conversation is created; deleting a conversation can cascade-delete its handoff feedback. This is request-triggered cleanup, not a background cron. Selected write paths may still operate on expired conversations. Acceptance must test a pending handoff for customer A past `CONVERSATION_TTL`, then a different customer B creating a conversation, and assert the pending case is retained; expired writes must be rejected or follow an explicitly approved policy rather than silently extending expiry.

### Confirmed R1 implementation blockers / conditional risks

1. **Profile-aware gateway instructions are required across provider paths.** Current Staff/Manager AI profiles do not exist. The reviewed OpenRouter path can prepend/append a customer/general system prompt to worker instructions; other provider adapters and Local/Remote paths must be inspected and tested independently rather than assumed to serialize the same payload. This is a **P0 blocker before internal profiles are enabled**, not proof that internal drafts have leaked today. Assert exact serialized prompts for every supported provider/profile.

2. **Checkpoint concern must be stated precisely.** Existing runs/checkpoints are already scoped by customer, purpose, request/conversation-derived run identity and lease/fencing; the claim that they are not bound to customer/request is not supported. The R1 gap is that identity/fingerprint/thread context does not yet distinguish internal actor, execution profile, selected case and projection version, and the schema requires a customer FK. Redesign actor-versus-subject attribution and profile/case checkpoint scope before routing internal profiles; do not create fake customer rows. This is a **conditional R1 design blocker**, not a confirmed current checkpoint collision.

3. **Cache isolation is incomplete for future profiles.** `SemanticCache` lookup is query-based without profile/case/projection dimensions; `ToolCache` keys do not encode all future profile/projection/case scope. This is not a demonstrated current cross-profile leak because those profiles are not implemented. Bypass internal-profile caches until keys and invalidation are proven safe. Separately, the product CRUD routes call `invalidate_product(pid)` positionally while its first parameter is `tenant_id`; this is a confirmed dormant signature bug on both refs and removes zero matching product-cache entries. It is dormant because `get_product` is not currently in `ToolCache.CACHEABLE_TOOLS`; product routes also clear SemanticCache/catalog revision through separate paths. Require keyword-argument invalidation and tests before enabling catalog caching.

4. **RAG scope and citation projection:** tenant-local schema/store scoping is present in the reviewed retrieval path; the audits found no basis to call it a cross-tenant vector/lexical leak. Retrieval is not bound to a selected handoff/case. Source results expose `citation_id`, `source_key`, `title`, `excerpt`, `content_sha256`, and `truncated`—not `source` or `version`. Current source permits up to 3 results and 1,100-character excerpts; stricter 1,000-character/3,000-total target caps may be used if labelled as R1 projection limits. `content_sha256` hashes the excerpt, not document version; `cited_sources` matches citation IDs against current-turn evidence but is not an entailment check. Decide whether R1 uses tenant-wide policy knowledge or case-specific evidence, and enforce any case/object restriction on the server.

5. **Provider request-mode behavior:** across provider adapters, `request_mode` can classify technical text containing terms such as `sql`, `database`, or `python` as `general` when no retail-intent term is present; `build_request` can then omit all tools and may choose `GENERAL_SYSTEM` when there is no explicit system message. `json` is not in `_GENERAL_TERMS` at the reviewed refs. Test this together with profile-aware instruction serialization on OpenRouterAgent's provider backends and LocalAgent/RemoteAgent paths. This is a conditional R1 risk, not evidence that an internal profile currently exists or leaks.

6. **Acceptance needs stronger observable assertions:** exact provider payload prompt/profile assertions including `request_mode`; executor-level tool denial; SemanticCache and ToolCache profile/case/projection tests; two concurrent case requests; atomic race tests among customer message, resolve and polling; pending handoff survival through tenant-wide expiry cleanup; exact serialized projection allowlists, types, enum and caps; Google role provisioning/`DEFAULT_ROLE`; Manager/admin route matrix; customer-safe event allow/deny; and fail-closed escalation persistence.

## 4. Architecture, docs and owner choices

- The shared-workflow architecture is preserved and remains the recommended direction. Do not split Customer, Staff and Manager into three graphs. Use immutable per-request profile context, backend authorization, profile-specific gateway instructions/tool capabilities/projections, and distinct output persistence where needed.
- The architecture Mermaid should distinguish turn-level SemanticCache lookup from ToolCache lookup inside the executor after authorization; every candidate output (cache hit, worker, direct Supervisor/fallback) must pass common output validation. The handoff sequence must show reply as non-terminal and make Staff `resolved` versus customer `customer_closed` mutually exclusive terminal transitions (`alt/else`) for one pending episode. It must also show gateway profile prompt, checkpoint identity, and scoped/bypassed cache behavior. Create/upsert the pending episode under a unique conversation/pending constraint first, then return the generated `handoff_id`; the client cannot key a new episode by an ID it does not yet have.
- Relative links in the reviewed R1 packet resolved. Root/current status/roadmap documents distinguish local tracking refs from deployment evidence. R1 source citations are primarily at `HEAD`; add baseline labels or paired line references where behavior differs on `origin/main`.
- This list records unresolved questions **at the time of the 2026-10-26 audit**. The historical 2026-10-28 snapshot and the current 2026-10-29 owner delta now specify gradual `platform_admin` retirement (separate from `opsadmin`), server-derived Google display identity, no client `staff_name` audit attribution, and case-bound context before vector retrieval. Remaining implementation decisions include retirement/quarantine migration mechanics, role-specific approval grants, claim/history/retention, evidence security, exact data/tool fields, production `/api/tools/execute`, cache policy, quota and internal draft/audit retention.
